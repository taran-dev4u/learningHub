📋 The problem

Houses in a row hold money, but robbing two adjacent houses trips the alarm; return the maximum you can rob.

Restated in our own words — the LeetCode icon above opens the formal statement with exact constraints and examples.

🧭 How to think about it

You are a professional robber planning to rob houses along a street. Each house has a certain amount of money stashed. Adjacent houses have security systems connected: it will automatically contact the police if two adjacent houses were broken into on the same night. Return the maximum amount of money you can rob tonight without alerting the police.

At each house i, you have two mutually exclusive choices:

  1. Rob house i: You earn nums[i], but you cannot rob house i - 1. Thus your total is nums[i] + max_loot_at(i - 2).
  2. Skip house i: You keep the maximum loot possible up to house i - 1: max_loot_at(i - 1).
The recurrence relation is: $$dp[i] = \max(dp[i - 1], dp[i - 2] + nums[i])$$

✅ The solution

Reveal the commented solution (attempt it first!) 
def rob(nums: list[int]) -> int:
    """
    Finds maximum loot without robbing adjacent houses in O(N) time and O(1) space.
    """
    rob1, rob2 = 0, 0
    # rob1: max money robbed up to i - 2
    # rob2: max money robbed up to i - 1
    for num in nums:
        curr = max(rob2, rob1 + num)
        rob1 = rob2
        rob2 = curr
        
    return rob2

⚡ Complexity analysis

  • Time Complexity: O(N). Single pass.
  • Space Complexity: O(1).
